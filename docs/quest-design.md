# Quest and character design

Lessons from playing and revising Der Fall Tilo. Each rule exists because breaking it
visibly hurt the story. The Director follows these along with `director-brief.md`.
`python/dm/stories/tilo.py` is the reference example.

## The quest

**Structure**
- Build the frame first: who did what, why, and where they are now. Check every line against it.
- Give the player a before. Someone met in ordinary life is missed when they vanish.
- Entangle the reasons. A choice should betray someone: reward against friendship, justice
  against mercy. An ending that costs nothing is not a decision.
- Consequences must happen: reward, changed attitudes, a secret delivered, people gone.
- Each ending and reward should be weighed in the game's economy. Equal options stay equal.

- A quest knows who the player is now. Someone too important is not drawn in: the world still
  moves on, but nobody brings it to them. A High Fire Mage is not sent after a runaway novice.

**Information**
- The player needs to learn that something happened, what it was, and why.
- Reach important facts through different approaches: asking someone who knows, tricking
  someone who would not tell, confronting the culprit, or finding evidence.
- An approach can close another. Revealing your interest burns the later deception.
- Everything must work out of order, including arriving knowing nothing. Cold visits offer
  hooks, not answers. Learning things in reverse needs its own lines.
- Once the outcome is decided, earlier scenes must no longer fire.
- Important information must be unmissable. State the reward outright, not in a figure of speech.

**The world must stay physically true**
- Changes happen where the player cannot see them. A person never vanishes in front of them.
- Time passes through sleep as well as waiting; a night that was slept through still happened.
- People and items belong on real waypoints and on the ground, never loose coordinates.
- A character's body matches the truth: someone who left his robe behind does not wear one.
- Clues never get the player in trouble for finding them.
- People who live or work next to someone have something to say about them, even if little.
- People stay reachable while the matter concerns them, and return to normal afterwards.

**How the player meets it**
- No markers. The journal records what the hero has found out, in his terse voice, with the
  game's own notice, and never tells the player what to do.
- Quest questions are asked once and then gone; the journal keeps the answer.
- NPCs start talking only if they can see the player and the player is free.
- Conversations can only be left from the main menu. Finished topics return there by themselves.
  "Zurück" appears only where more can still be asked. Stories may still end a talk deliberately.

## The characters

**Fill out only what can happen.** A sheet covers the situations this story can actually reach.
For a quest limited to novices and mages, nobody needs a view of the hero as a paladin. A prefilled
story does this in advance; a live Director fills them in from the game state it is handed.

**Every character has a sheet**: role, core, background, manner of speech, and attitudes to
factions, important people, those around them, politics and game state, plus how those change.
A line that could belong to anyone is filler.

**People speak from what they know and want**
- An NPC knows only what could have reached them. So does the hero: he can only say what the
  player has learned, so specific lines are gated and a vague alternative is offered.
- Every line answers what was actually just said. Nobody responds to a question not asked.
- Name a person before calling them "er", on every path.
- Keep the speaker's situation in mind: who they are talking to, where they are, what has
  already happened. A debt that was paid is no longer owed; a man hiding in an alley is no
  regular at the bar; a fellow novice is no stranger and will recognise you.
- Characters do not know the player's private decisions unless told. Let the hero tell them.
- First meetings need a shorter line for later ones.

**Voice**
- Rough, colloquial German in the style of the original game. Keep attitude in every line.
- Generate from a long reference of several ordinary lines; use real lines matching a delivery
  for emotion, such as threats for "drohend". Reference lines for one person should come from
  one sound, or the depth shifts between lines.
- Cloned voices stay private on this machine and never ship.
